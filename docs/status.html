Status

COMMAND is a consent-driven Android alarm and execution director: it rings for a task, asks for an honest answer, gives you a first step and a five-minute starter, checks in, and keeps your own record of what happened. Everything stays on the phone. The from-scratch rebuild (reconstruction design) has reached the end of its essentials: what is built is below, with what was cut and is coming back, and what has never run on a real phone. The release receipt covers the build; the pilot protocol is the owner's next step.

Cut on 2026-11-06, coming back

On 2026-11-06 the owner cut the optional work to finish the core alarm loop (amendment 35). On 2026-11-07 the owner said: "Once everything concludes, add these back to the maximum capabilities." The release receipt was the last essential, so all of this now returns, each milestone reading its interfaces from the code first (amendment 39):

  • The QR handshake: built, not yet checked on a phone (M13a). NFC and voice handshakes (M13b, M13c) follow, which bring P1's seven verified handshakes; today there are four verified.
  • Gemini directive text and Studio voices (M14a, M14b); today speech is the phone's own engine, so the ten packs differ by pitch, rate and installed voice.
  • Opt-in cloud sync (M15a-1, M15a-2, M15b) and P2's launchers and suggested first actions (M16).
  • From the voice screen: custom packs, A/B comparison, the template editor, and the voice and preset steps of onboarding. From M12: the Smaller recovery option, per-mission lock policy and "Skip this one". Screens to create, rename and delete a profile.
  • The second device checkpoint (DC2) and a second release receipt after it.

Because they return, the Firebase libraries and the INTERNET permission stay in the build. No code that runs today uses the network (NoNetworkInRuntimeCodeTest).

Never run on a real phone

Every check so far ran on emulators, which have no speaker and no phone maker's battery manager. Still owed, by the owner on their own phone (issue #4, and the listening of issue #2):

  • A real phone ringing on time, including after a restart and after days of standby.
  • The voice heard through a real speaker at the loudness targets, including the backup male voice for Stone.
  • Headset and Bluetooth routing.
  • Phone makers' battery killers and auto-start settings.
  • TalkBack on a real phone (DC1 could only read it on an emulator).
