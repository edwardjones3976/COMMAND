Version: 3.1 · Issued: October 29, 2026 · Status: Owner-aligned design and proposed implementation requirements, not a completion or deployment receipt.

Core promise: A consent-driven, Android-first personal execution director that gets someone from an existing schedule to a real, self-reported task start through forceful alarms, deliberate acknowledgment, spoken instructions, simple follow-up and humane recovery. 1. Problem, audience, and market research

Primary personal problem: The owner can create detailed recurring calendars, but repeated phone reminders have not reliably caused activities to begin. A schedule is not the same as task execution. COMMAND is for people with plans who dismiss notifications, procrastinate, or struggle with transitions. Do not characterize users as clinically diagnosed or permanently label them "cheaters."

Direct competitors and what is already solved: Forfeit / Overlord advertises 24/7 accountability, consequences, device/app controls, and evidence; Alarmy offers demanding wake-up missions (including QR, typing and chained challenges) and follow-up; Routinery provides guided routines; Tiimo offers planning/focus support; Focusmate provides human coworking accountability. These are competitor-reported capabilities, not independently demonstrated comparative outcomes. Customizable coaching, chained alarms and task follow-up are not inherently novel.

Proposed differentiation to test: the complete, low-friction alarm → handshake → immediate action → lightweight check-in loop without compulsory photos, financial punishment, constant tracking or evidence paperwork. Allow strong, deliberately obnoxious coaching while keeping the user in charge. Focus on sustained meaningful task starts rather than alarms dismissed.

Market velocity (distinct from coding speed): track how quickly incumbents add integrated guided starts and penalty-free assertive coaching. Re-check official product changelogs before material investment. Risk is high because the category and many components already exist; no unsupported market share or buyer probability claims.

2. Definitive interaction cycle

  1. Commitment: User selects or imports an event, task label, start time, recurrence, priority, and action instruction; rigid and flexible blocks remain distinct.
  2. Attention capture: At the scheduled time, COMMAND triggers the user's approved alarm/voice/vibration policy within the device's real capabilities.
  3. Handshake: User completes an approved deliberate acknowledgement challenge or uses a safety/escape action that records the true outcome.
  4. Immediate direction: Speak one concise instruction with the next physical or digital step. Offer a five-minute starter; opening the project or appropriate app can be optional.
  5. Follow-through: At a configured short interval, ask a simple status question. Answers: Started, Still working, Done, Need a reset, Delay, Skip. Each is user-reported; no verification demand.
  6. Recovery: Offer one bounded restart, smaller next step or approved reschedule; log unconfirmed if no response. Protect sleep, leisure, urgent interruptions, and user-initiated silence.
Nonnegotiable state separation: Alarm acknowledged ≠ task started ≠ meaningful progress ≠ task completed. An alarm challenge never awards task-completion credit. Equally, self-reported completion is permitted without evidence.

Coaching personalities: Drill Sergeant (firm, loud, humorously impatient and deliberately a little obnoxious), Executive Director (precise/professional), Supportive Coach, Accountability Partner, Minimalist, and Custom. Personality controls wording, voice, repetition and tone; it does not grant permissions. No insults, degradation, harassment, humiliation or unsafe commands.

Separate user-reported 1–5 dimensions: instruction firmness; obnoxiousness; alert intensity; follow-up persistence; handshake difficulty; schedule rigidity; and recovery structure. No unexplained composite seriousness or inferred diagnosis. A gentle voice plus difficult QR challenge must be possible; so must obnoxious voice plus a single alert. Privacy, money, account access, tracking and partner notification require separate opt-in, not implicit "MAX" activation.

Example: "HEY! YOU FINISHED THE HANDSHAKE. FANTASTIC. NOW GET TO YOUR COMPUTER, OPEN THE PROJECT AND GIVE ME FIVE MINUTES. MOVE!" Users select/edit frequency and tone.

5. Mission Integrity — deliberately simplified

No mandatory Mission Receipts in V1. No photo proof, code diffs, study tests, workout documentation, surveillance, compulsory time logs, phone/app monitoring or financial stakes. One-tap statuses are sufficient. Future proof-supported modes may only be added as independent, revocable, opt-in functionality; never as the meaning of "MAX."

For repeated acknowledgment without reported starts: vary the immediate instruction, offer a tiny first action, propose a 5-minute starter or shorten an ineffective challenge. Do not automatically intensify loudness or hurdles. More challenge is not always more progress.

State model: SCHEDULED → ARMED → FIRING → ACKNOWLEDGED / SAFETY_SILENCED / MISSED → GUIDED_START → STARTED_SELF_REPORTED / BLOCKED / DELAYED / SKIPPED / UNCONFIRMED → WORKING_SELF_REPORTED / COMPLETED_SELF_REPORTED / RECOVERY_OFFERED. Keep orthogonal event history (alarm fired, challenge attempt, permission revocation, follow-up sent, status change) so status transitions are auditable. Pending alarms are deduplicated by mission occurrence ID; reboots/time-zone and DST changes are reconciled.

6. Calendar, Mission Lock and scheduling policy

Start with manual missions, then read-only Android calendar and Google Calendar where explicitly authorized. Import only selected calendars. Preserve provider event IDs, last-sync time, recurrence exceptions and time zone; avoid duplicate notifications. Never write back without an additional grant. The user can use rigid Monday–Thursday routines and controlled flexibility Friday–Sunday. Protect rest, optional blocks, quiet time, driving, emergencies, calendar overrides and appropriately designated leisure.

Mission Lock: user-configured friction before abandoning a fixed commitment; e.g. confirmation, short reason selection or offered recovery. Not an unstoppable alarm or device lockdown. All changes remain revocable, and the user may pause, cancel, or use emergency silence. Record skipped as skipped, not as completed.

10. Market go/no-go, ownership and scope

Primary KPI: improvement in useful self-reported task starts and sustained task follow-through at an acceptable interruption burden. Alarm dismissal and challenge success are diagnostics, not main outcomes. Compare against a credible Alarmy/Routinery-style baseline and Overlord where feasible, not only a weak default notification.

Go: repeatable improvement for a defined cohort; Revise: acknowledgments increase but starts do not; Hold/narrow: repeated counterproductive stress or poor usefulness. Serving a reliably benefited niche is sufficient; mass appeal is not required.

Non-goals: force obedience, prevent legitimate cancellation, punitive fines, public humiliation, mandatory proof, deep device surveillance, recreation of a full calendar, universal alarm reliability guarantees or new foundation-model development.
